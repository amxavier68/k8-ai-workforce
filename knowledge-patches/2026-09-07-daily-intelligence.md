## 1. AI-generated, inferred and momentarily held personal information can be collection under APP 3

Source: Office of the Australian Information Commissioner, APP Guidelines Chapter 3 version 1.2, updated 2026-05-13: https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-3-app-3-collection-of-solicited-personal-information

Affected leads: Renee, Sentinel, Atlas, Kai, DevMate

Verified change: OAIC guidance states that personal information created with reference to, or generated, inferred or observed from, information an entity holds is a collection to which APP 3 applies. Examples include AI, automated decision making, analytics, cookies, website agents and chatbots. Public availability does not remove APP obligations, and momentary holding can still be collection.

Operational effect: when an APP entity or otherwise covered K8/client workflow uses AI, scraping, tracking, enrichment, chatbots or derived profiles, assess collection purpose, necessity, proportionality, data minimisation, consent where required, third-party handling and retention. Do not assume that public, inferred, transient or machine-generated data falls outside privacy governance. Determine Privacy Act coverage and obtain legal advice where necessary; this record is an operating safeguard, not legal advice.

## 2. Automated-decision privacy-policy obligations commence 10 December 2026 for APP entities

Source: Office of the Australian Information Commissioner, APP Guidelines Chapter 1 version 1.2: https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-1-app-1-open-and-transparent-management-of-personal-information

Affected leads: Renee, Sentinel, Atlas, Clarity, DevMate

Verified change: from 2026-12-10, APP 1.7–1.9 require an APP entity to include specified information in its APP Privacy Policy when it has arranged for a computer program to use personal information to make, or do something substantially and directly related to making, a decision that could reasonably be expected to significantly affect an individual's rights or interests.

Operational effect: treat 2026-12-10 as a compliance gate, not a blanket claim that every score, recommendation or automation is covered. Before changing a K8 or client privacy policy, establish APP-entity coverage, map the actual personal information, program role and decision effect, and determine whether the statutory threshold is met. Preserve explainability and human escalation where decisions could be consequential. No policy publication, legal conclusion or client representation is authorised by this patch.

## 3. Google Search generative-AI performance reports are globally available

Source: Google Search Central, published 2026-06-03 and rollout note updated 2026-08-31: https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/06/gen-ai-performance-reports

Affected leads: Renee, Kai, Atlas, Clarity

Verified change: Google states that dedicated Search Console reports for visibility in generative AI features were rolled out to all websites worldwide by 2026-08-31. The reports expose impressions, pages, countries, devices and dates for eligible AI features in Search and Discover.

Operational effect: first-party Search Console reporting becomes the primary Google evidence source for observed visibility in Google generative AI features when the property and report are accessible. Treat impressions as exposure evidence, not proof of citation, conversion, ranking causation or the effectiveness of a specific optimisation. Do not alter K8 Scan scoring, weights or public claims until a bounded dataset and validation rule are reviewed and owner-approved.
